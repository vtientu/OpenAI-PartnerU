# 05 — Talking About AI Transformation Without Overclaiming

> **Course:** Foundation — OpenAI Foundational Knowledge  
> **Course:** How AI is Driving Business Transformation  
> **Section:** Talking about AI transformation without overclaiming
>
> **Goal:** Giúp partner nói về AI transformation theo cách compelling nhưng vẫn chính xác, evidence-based và có credibility.

---

# 1. Section objective

Sau section này, bạn cần:

- phân biệt **possibility, evidence và guarantee**;
- biết cách dùng external AI research đúng cách;
- tránh hype khi nói productivity, jobs, ROI và transformation;
- dùng customer-specific hypothesis + validation;
- biết cấu trúc một transformation story có value nhưng không overclaim.

---

# 2. Big picture

Partner cần tránh hai cực:

```text
HYPE
"AI changes everything immediately"

vs.

UNDERSELL
"AI is just another productivity tool"
```

Cách tốt hơn:

```text
Capability
+ Evidence
+ Customer context
+ Clear assumptions
+ Measurement plan
=
Credible transformation story
```

---

# 3. Overclaiming là gì?

**Overclaiming** = statement mạnh hơn evidence.

Ví dụ:

### Evidence

> Một report cho thấy surveyed users báo cáo tiết kiệm thời gian.

### Overclaim

> “AI sẽ tiết kiệm mỗi employee của customer đúng từng đó thời gian.”

Sai vì external observation ≠ customer guarantee.

---

# 4. Four claim levels

Hãy phân biệt:

## Level 1 — Capability

> “AI can summarize long documents.”

## Level 2 — Observed evidence

> “In OpenAI's enterprise report, surveyed workers reported X type of benefit.”

## Level 3 — Customer hypothesis

> “In this workflow, we expect summarization may reduce preparation effort.”

## Level 4 — Customer evidence

> “The pilot measured a 28% reduction in preparation time.”

Level 4 mạnh nhất cho customer.

Partner không nên nhảy từ Level 1 → certainty.

---

# 5. Possibility ≠ probability ≠ outcome

Ba câu khác nhau:

> AI **can** automate part of this workflow.

> AI is **likely** to create value here.

> AI **created** value of X.

Câu 1 là capability.

Câu 2 cần evidence/assumptions.

Câu 3 cần measurement.

Communication discipline cần giữ chúng tách biệt.

---

# 6. External research — cách dùng đúng

OpenAI reports có thể hỗ trợ:

- show broader adoption;
- identify patterns;
- demonstrate examples;
- form hypotheses.

Không nên dùng để:

- guarantee ROI;
- predict exact customer savings;
- claim all workers/functions benefit equally.

Good wording:

> “OpenAI's 2025 enterprise report observed reported time savings and department-specific improvements among surveyed users. We should validate the equivalent impact in your workflow through a pilot.”

---

# 7. Be precise about population and date

Một claim tốt gồm:

```text
Who?
When?
What was measured?
How?
```

Example:

Bad:

> “75% of workers are more productive with AI.”

Better:

> “In OpenAI's 2025 enterprise report, surveyed enterprise users reported specific speed/quality benefits; this evidence reflects that sample and time period.”

Exact statistic chỉ dùng khi cần và khi source được kiểm tra.

---

# 8. Avoid universal statements

Words to watch:

- always;
- every;
- guaranteed;
- eliminates;
- replaces;
- no risk;
- fully autonomous;
- 100%.

Không phải cấm tuyệt đối trong mọi sentence, nhưng chúng thường signal claim quá rộng.

---

# 9. Talking about productivity

Weak:

> “AI makes employees 30% more productive.”

Questions:

- Which employees?
- Which task?
- Which metric?
- Which study?
- Productivity defined how?

Better:

> “AI can reduce effort in research, drafting, analysis and coding workflows. We should measure task time, quality and throughput for the teams in scope.”

---

# 10. Talking about ROI

ROI không đến từ capability alone.

Mental model:

```text
ROI
=
Realized business benefit
-
Total cost
```

Total cost có thể gồm:

- technology;
- implementation;
- integration;
- change management;
- operations;
- review/oversight.

Benefit có thể gồm:

- time saved;
- cost avoided;
- increased revenue;
- capacity;
- quality.

Không nói ROI nếu chưa define numerator/denominator.

---

# 11. Talking about automation

Overclaim:

> “This process can be fully automated.”

Better:

> “Several steps appear automatable; we should measure exception rate and identify where human approval remains appropriate.”

Automation nên được nói như spectrum.

---

# 12. Talking about jobs

Tránh simplistic statements:

> “AI will replace job X.”

Jobs = bundles of tasks.

Better framing:

> “AI can change the task mix within a role—automating some steps, augmenting others, and potentially creating new responsibilities.”

OpenAI 2026 research also observed cross-occupation task expansion among ChatGPT users.

Điểm đó support conversation về work redesign, không phải deterministic job prediction.

---

# 13. Talking about industries

Overclaim:

> “Finance is the best industry for AI.”

Không có universal ranking cần thiết.

Better:

> “OpenAI enterprise data shows meaningful adoption in finance, while customer-specific value depends on workflow, data, controls and readiness.”

Industry trend is context.

Not verdict.

---

# 14. Talking about model capabilities

Models evolve quickly.

Avoid static claim:

> “Model X is the best for all reasoning.”

Use:

> “For this workflow, we should evaluate candidate models on quality, latency, cost and controls.”

Capability claims nên version-specific khi cần.

---

# 15. Transformation language ladder

Có thể dùng ladder từ evidence thấp → cao.

## Exploratory

> “AI may help…”

## Capability-backed

> “Current models can…”

## Evidence-backed

> “Public/customer evidence shows…”

## Validated

> “Our pilot measured…”

## Operational

> “Production metrics show…”

Mức confidence tăng cùng evidence.

---

# 16. A credible transformation story

Dùng 6 phần:

```text
1. Business problem
2. Workflow today
3. AI capability
4. Workflow change
5. Expected/measured value
6. Evidence + next validation
```

---

# 17. Example — Sales

Bad:

> “AI will revolutionize sales and dramatically increase revenue.”

Credible:

> “Sales reps currently spend significant time on account research and follow-up administration. AI can synthesize account context, draft follow-ups and prepare CRM updates. The initial hypothesis is reduced prep/admin time and faster follow-up; a pilot should measure time saved, CRM completeness and conversion-related metrics.”

---

# 18. Example — Engineering

Bad:

> “AI developers will write all code.”

Credible:

> “AI coding tools can assist with code understanding, implementation, testing and debugging. The impact varies by task and codebase, so engineering teams should measure lead time, review effort and defect quality rather than assume a fixed productivity gain.”

---

# 19. Example — Customer support

Bad:

> “AI can automate 90% of support.”

Unless exact validated evidence exists, this is risky.

Better:

> “The workflow contains repeated knowledge and classification steps that appear suitable for AI. We can segment ticket categories, evaluate quality, and determine which low-risk cases can be automated versus escalated.”

---

# 20. Evidence triangle

Strong customer narrative combines:

```text
Public evidence
       /\
      /  \
Product   Customer
evidence   evidence
```

### Public evidence

Research/report.

### Product evidence

System docs/evals.

### Customer evidence

Pilot/production.

Customer evidence usually matters most for final business claim.

---

# 21. Hypothesis-first communication

Partner doesn't need certainty to progress opportunity.

Instead:

> “Our hypothesis is…”

Example:

```text
Hypothesis:
AI-assisted research can reduce seller preparation time.

Evidence needed:
baseline prep time
pilot prep time
quality score
seller adoption
```

Hypothesis is honest and actionable.

---

# 22. Measurement language

Use wording:

- baseline;
- target;
- pilot;
- measured;
- observed;
- sample;
- representative cases;
- confidence;
- exception;
- trade-off.

This vocabulary encourages evidence-based transformation.

---

# 23. Separate technical success from business success

Technical:

> model achieves acceptable evaluation score.

Business:

> process cycle time improves.

Adoption:

> users use the system.

All three matter.

```text
Technical success
× Adoption
× Workflow impact
=
Realized business value
```

---

# 24. Avoid case-study transfer error

Customer A achieved outcome X.

Does not mean Customer B will.

Differences:

- workflow;
- baseline;
- data;
- people;
- scale;
- integration;
- market.

Use case study as:

> proof that something is possible.

Not:

> guarantee of same outcome.

---

# 25. A safe and strong sentence template

Use:

> **“We’ve seen [evidence/pattern]. In your environment, the relevant hypothesis is [customer value]. We would validate that through [pilot/eval/metric].”**

Example:

> “We’ve seen enterprises use AI to reduce repetitive research and drafting effort. In your sales workflow, the hypothesis is shorter account-prep time. We would validate that by comparing baseline and pilot preparation time and quality.”

---

# 26. Transformation ≠ full automation

Transformation can be:

- better human capability;
- fewer handoffs;
- faster decisions;
- new product;
- higher service capacity;
- different role boundaries.

Don't equate:

```text
Transformation
=
remove humans
```

Often transformation is:

```text
Human + AI + workflow redesign
```

---

# 27. How to talk to executives

Executive story should be:

```text
Business issue
→ scale
→ AI opportunity
→ impact hypothesis
→ dependencies
→ validation
```

Not:

```text
Model benchmark
→ context window
→ token details
```

Unless CTO/technical audience asks.

---

# 28. How to talk to technical teams

Technical audience needs:

- architecture;
- data;
- evals;
- latency;
- security;
- controls;
- integration.

Avoid purely inspirational story.

Transformation requires implementation credibility.

---

# 29. How to say “I don't know”

Good partner phrase:

> “I don't want to turn that into a commitment without checking the current product documentation / customer evidence.”

This increases credibility.

Overclaiming to avoid temporary uncertainty can create larger trust damage later.

---

# 30. Claim checklist — VERIFY

Mnemonic:

## V — Version
Is product/model/version clear?

## E — Evidence
What supports the claim?

## R — Range
Does claim apply only in a scope/population?

## I — Interpretation
Am I separating fact from inference?

## F — Fit
Does evidence fit this customer/use case?

## Y — Your next validation
What still needs to be tested?

---

# 31. Common mistakes

## Mistake 1
Use case study result as guarantee.

## Mistake 2
Convert capability into ROI claim.

## Mistake 3
Talk job replacement instead of tasks.

## Mistake 4
Use old model facts as current.

## Mistake 5
Use “transformation” with no workflow change.

## Mistake 6
Hide uncertainty.

## Mistake 7
No measurement plan.

# 33. Section recap

1. Capability ≠ guaranteed customer outcome.
2. Separate capability, observed evidence, customer hypothesis and customer proof.
3. State population/date/context for research claims.
4. Transformation is broader than automation.
5. Jobs should be discussed at task/workflow level.
6. Case study = possibility/evidence, not guarantee.
7. Use baseline + pilot + metrics.
8. Strong partner language:
   **“We’ve seen X. Our hypothesis for you is Y. We’ll validate with Z.”**
